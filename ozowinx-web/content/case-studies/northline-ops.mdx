## Challenge

Operations planning ran on a chain of spreadsheets emailed between five regional
teams. Every planning cycle took most of a week, and by the time the numbers were
consolidated they were already out of date. Nobody could answer a simple question
— what is the current plan? — without asking three people first.

## Approach

We started by mapping the real process rather than the documented one, including
the informal steps teams had invented to work around the tooling. That surfaced
the actual constraint: not the spreadsheets themselves, but the absence of a
single record that everyone trusted.

## Architecture

A multi-tenant Next.js application over PostgreSQL, with row-level security
enforcing regional boundaries and an append-only revision log so any plan can be
reconstructed as it stood on a given date.

## Outcome

The planning cycle compressed from five days to a single afternoon. More
importantly, every team now works from the same figures, and disagreements are
about the plan rather than about whose spreadsheet is correct.

## Lessons Learned

The hardest part was not the software. It was agreeing which team owned each
number — a governance question the tooling had allowed everyone to avoid.
