There is a pattern to AI projects that do not ship. The prototype works. The demo
is convincing. Then the project spends six months not reaching production, and
quietly stops being mentioned.

The cause is usually not the model. It is that the prototype was built against a
curated extract, and production requires the real data — which is inconsistent,
partially duplicated, and governed by access rules nobody wrote down.

## The boundary problem

A model is only as useful as the data it can legitimately see. In a prototype
that boundary is imaginary; in production it is a hard constraint enforced by
compliance, contracts, and access control. Teams that treat the boundary as a
deployment detail discover late that half their retrieval corpus cannot be used.

## What to do differently

Establish the data boundary first. Before evaluating models, answer: what data can
this system see, for which users, under what conditions, and who signs off on that.
The answer constrains the architecture, and it is far cheaper to learn early.

The projects that ship are the ones where this question was asked in week one
rather than month six.
